The general concept of building a PBPK model has previously been described by Kuepfer et al. ([Kuepfer 2016](#main-references)). Information regarding the relevant anthropometric (height, weight) and physiological parameters (e.g. blood flows, organ volumes, binding protein concentrations, hematocrit, cardiac output) in adults was gathered from the literature and has been previously published ([PK-Sim Ontogeny Database Version 7.3](#main-references)). The information was incorporated into PK-Sim® and was used as default values for the simulations in adults.

The applied activity and variability of plasma proteins and active processes that are integrated into PK-Sim® are described in the publicly available PK-Sim® Ontogeny Database Version 7.3 ([Schlender 2016](#main-references)) or otherwise referenced for the specific process.

The model was initially taken as a modified version of the morphine and M6G PBPK model published by Schaefer et al. ([Schaefer 2018](#main-references)), whose structure and initial parameterization had been established by fitting to intravenous morphine and M6G plasma concentration-time data from Lötsch et al. ([Lötsch 2002](#main-references)). This model was extended to include the second glucuronide metabolite, M3G, and additional intravenous clinical datasets. The base mean model was built and evaluated using clinical data from intravenous single-dose and short-infusion studies of morphine, M6G, and M3G to refine an appropriate structure to describe the pharmacokinetics of morphine and its metabolites in plasma. The mean PBPK model was developed using typical European individuals with demographics matching those of the clinical studies. Hepatic metabolism of morphine is described as two first-order UGT2B7 pathways forming M6G and M3G, respectively, and a minor first-order CYP3A4 pathway. The relative tissue-specific expression of these enzymes was taken from the PK-Sim gene expression database. M6G and M3G are eliminated renally by glomerular filtration of the unbound fraction, with M6G additionally undergoing active tubular secretion.

The metabolic clearances of morphine (see [Section 2.3](#model-parameters-and-assumptions)) were identified using the Parameter Identification module provided in PK-Sim®, fitted simultaneously against the plasma concentration-time profiles of morphine, M6G, and M3G in the model-building dataset ([Säwe 1985](#main-references), [Crews 2001](#main-references)). Structural model selection was mainly guided by visual inspection of the resulting description of the data and biological plausibility.

Overall, the model was built and evaluated for intravenous bolus and infusion administration of morphine at doses ranging from 3.55 to 7.59 mg. The model was then verified by simulating morphine plasma concentrations after intravenous bolus and infusion administration within this dose range, including a weight-normalized bolus-plus-infusion regimen of 0.124+0.177 mg/kg and a combined regimen with concurrently administered M6G ([Lötsch 2002](#main-references), [Westerling 1995](#main-references), [Hoskin 1989](#main-references), [Lötsch 1998](#main-references), [Dershwitz 2000](#main-references), [Stuart-Harris 2000](#main-references), [Baillie 1989](#main-references), [Osborne 1990](#main-references), [Ferslew 2015](#main-references)). M6G plasma concentrations were verified after direct intravenous administration of 1 mg and weight-normalized bolus-plus-infusion regimens ranging from 0.015+0.0288 to 0.044+0.088 mg/kg, and as the metabolite formed from the administered morphine doses above ([Lötsch 2002](#main-references), [Lötsch 1998](#main-references), [Westerling 1995](#main-references), [Osborne 1990](#main-references), [Stuart-Harris 2000](#main-references)). M3G plasma concentrations were verified as the metabolite formed from the administered morphine doses above ([Osborne 1990](#main-references), [Ferslew 2015](#main-references), [Stuart-Harris 2000](#main-references)).

Details about input data (physicochemical, *in vitro* and clinical) can be found in [Section 2.2](#methods-data).

Details about the model and its parameters can be found in [Section 2.3](#model-parameters-and-assumptions).
