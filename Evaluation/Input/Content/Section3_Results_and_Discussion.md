The PBPK model for morphine, morphine-6-glucuronide (M6G), and morphine-3-glucuronide (M3G) was developed and verified with clinical intravenous pharmacokinetic data.

The model was evaluated using clinical intravenous pharmacokinetic data from eleven studies ([Säwe 1985](#main-references), [Crews 2001](#main-references), [Lötsch 2002](#main-references), [Westerling 1995](#main-references), [Hoskin 1989](#main-references), [Lötsch 1998](#main-references), [Dershwitz 2000](#main-references), [Stuart-Harris 2000](#main-references), [Baillie 1989](#main-references), [Osborne 1990](#main-references), [Ferslew 2015](#main-references)). The model was evaluated for morphine intravenous bolus and infusion administration in a dose range of 3.55 to 7.59 mg, including a weight-normalized bolus-plus-infusion regimen of 0.124+0.177 mg/kg; for M6G following direct intravenous administration of 1 mg and weight-normalized bolus-plus-infusion regimens ranging from 0.015+0.0288 to 0.044+0.088 mg/kg, as well as formation from the administered morphine doses; and for M3G as the metabolite formed from morphine doses of 3.55 to 7.52 mg.

The model quantifies the plasma pharmacokinetics of morphine and its two glucuronide metabolites, M6G and M3G, following intravenous bolus and infusion administration of morphine (and, in one study, of M6G itself), covering hepatic glucuronidation via UGT2B7, minor CYP3A4 metabolism, and renal elimination of M6G and M3G by glomerular filtration (GFR) and active tubular secretion of M6G.
