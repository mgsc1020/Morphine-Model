Morphine is an opioid analgesic that is commonly used to treat moderate to severe acute and chronic pain, and is still considered the "gold standard" in pain management care and other medical settings ([Coates 2024](#main-references)). It is a potent agonist at the µ-opioid receptor (MOR) and serves as the reference substance against which the analgesic potency of other opioids is measured ([Pathan 2012](#main-references)). Clinical uses include post-operative, cancer-related, and palliative care pain management ([Mugabure Bujedo 2012](#main-references)).

After intravenous administration, morphine distributes into a large volume of distribution (1 to 4.7 L/kg) and is approximately 35 % bound to plasma proteins ([Olsen 1975](#main-references), [Baxter 2005](#main-references)). Morphine is considered to be mainly eliminated in the liver with a minor contribution of renal excretion. Morphine is extensively metabolized to morphine-3-glucuronide (M3G) and morphine-6-glucuronide (M6G) predominantly by UDP-glucuronosyltransferase 2B7 (UGT2B7) as well as other UGT enzymes ([Hasselström 1993](#main-references), [Crews 2001](#main-references), [Coffman 1997](#main-references)). A minor fraction of the dose undergoes N-demethylation to normorphine, primarily via cytochrome P450 3A4 (CYP3A4) ([Schaefer 2018](#main-references), [Projean 2003a](#main-references)). M6G is an active metabolite that contributes to the analgesic profile of morphine while M3G lacks analgesic activity ([Projean 2003b](#main-references)). Approximately 10 % of the dose is excreted unchanged in urine, and M3G and M6G are eliminated renally ([Hasselström 1993](#main-references), [Crews 2001](#main-references)). This model describes the plasma concentration-time profiles of morphine, M3G, and M6G following intravenous administration (bolus and short infusion) in healthy adults.

The herein presented model building and evaluation report evaluates the performance of the physiologically based pharmacokinetic (PBPK) model for morphine in (healthy) adults.

The presented morphine PBPK model as well as the respective evaluation plan and evaluation report are provided open-source ([https://github.com/Open-Systems-Pharmacology/Morphine-Model](https://github.com/Open-Systems-Pharmacology/Morphine-Model)).
